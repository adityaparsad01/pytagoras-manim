Why does a squared plus b squared equal c squared?

Start with a right triangle. Its two shorter sides are a and b, and the hypotenuse is c.

Build a square on each side. Their areas are a squared, b squared, and c squared.

For a three, four, five triangle, that becomes nine plus sixteen equals twenty-five.

Now imagine a larger square with side a plus b. Put four copies of the triangle inside it. The remaining center is a square with area c squared.

The large square has area a squared plus two ab plus b squared. The four triangles contribute two ab.

Cancel two ab from both sides, and we get the Pythagorean theorem: a squared plus b squared equals c squared.
