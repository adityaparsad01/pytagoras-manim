Why does a squared plus b squared equal c squared?

Start with a right triangle. The shorter sides are a and b, and the hypotenuse is c.

Build a square on each side. Their areas are a squared, b squared, and c squared.

For a three, four, five triangle, that gives nine plus sixteen equals twenty-five.

Now make it general. Put four identical triangles inside a larger square with side a plus b.

The large square has area a squared plus two a b plus b squared. The four triangles use two a b, leaving a center square with area c squared.

Cancel two a b, and we get a squared plus b squared equals c squared.

That is the Pythagorean theorem.
